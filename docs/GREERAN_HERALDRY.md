## 0. How arms actually work (why this matters here)

Two facts from authoritative heraldic practice shape everything below [refs: 2,3]:

1. **Coats of arms are granted to individuals**, not to a surname, and descend to
   that person's own descendants. There is therefore no single "the Greeran coat
   of arms," "the King coat of arms," etc. Hundreds of unrelated families share a
   surname; each may have distinct (or no) arms.
2. **Surname "family crest / coat of arms" products sold online are not authentic
   heraldry.** They are, at best, arms of *some* historical bearer of the name —
   not yours unless you can prove direct descent from that bearer, which for these
   lines is generally not established. In Ireland, arms are likewise granted to an
   individual; the state authority is the Genealogical Office (Chief Herald).

Consequence for "seize quartiers": the classical nobiliary term means *proving
sixteen quarters of **nobility***. In this project the 16 great-great-grandparent
slots are filled with **documented commoners** (farmers, emigrants, craftsmen), so
the achievement is **genealogical completeness** — it does **not** confer nobility
or armigerous status, and no noble-arms claim should be drawn from it.

## 2. Heraldry / arms — status per line (the honest mapping)

### 2a. Irish paternal surnames (Greeran/Greenan, King, Doyle, Rooney, Conalty, Hillan, Dogherty, McManus — Co. Down)
- **Authentic arms for *these specific ancestors*: none documented.**
- These were Gaelic/Catholic smallholder and labouring families of county Down.
  Gaelic tenant-farming families were generally **not armigerous** — they did not
  hold grants of arms. There is no primary heraldic record tying any of these named
  ancestors (Bernard Greeran 1831, Mary Conalty, John Hillan, Hugh King, Bridget
  Doyle, etc.) to a coat of arms.
- Surname-derived shields exist in commercial armorials (e.g., generic "Doyle,"
  "King," "O'Doherty" images), but per §0 these belong to specific other bearers/
  septs and are **not** claimable for this line without unbroken, documented male-
  line descent — which is neither established nor asserted here. I will not present
  them as the family's arms.

### 2b. American/English-origin surnames (Carey, Stanley, Bickford, Worth, Hewett, Thornburg, Morin)
- Same rule applies. Each of these surnames has armorial history somewhere (e.g.,
  the **Stanley** arms belong to the Earls of Derby line; the many Irish **Ó Ciardha
  Carey** arms attach to specific septs). But the documented bearers here — e.g.,
  Elizabeth **Stanley** b. 1831 Indiana, or Joel **Thornburg** Carey — show **no
  evidence of connection** to those armigerous families. Assigning them the famous
  arms would be a false claim. Correct position: **no authentic arms map onto these
  ancestors as documented.**

### 2c. The one genuinely "impressive," documentable lineage credential
- Descendants of **Samuel Carey (1752–1823), NSDAR Ancestor #A020262**, qualify for
  the **Daughters/Sons of the American Revolution** (DAR/SAR) on documented
  Revolutionary War service (2nd-class Private, Capt. Robert Gibson's Co., Plumstead
  Twp Militia, 1780–81; PA Archives 5th Ser. vol.5, pp.369–370) [1,2].
- **Important distinction:** DAR/SAR membership is a **patriotic lineage honor
  based on proven military service** — it is *not* a coat of arms and confers no
  armigerous status. It is, however, the strongest, fully-provable "ancestry
  credential" this family holds, and it is the honest headline of this research.

### 2d. Military-service lineage (documented, unrelated to arms)
