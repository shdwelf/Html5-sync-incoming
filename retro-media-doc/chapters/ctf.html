CAPTURE THE FLAG cybersecurity as a competitive sport, and the archive that tracks it.

Chapter 06 — Capture The Flag & CTFtime

Capture the Flag (CTF) is a cybersecurity competition where teams solve challenges to find hidden "flags" — short strings like flag{...} — buried in intentionally vulnerable programs, websites, files or systems. CTFs are how a huge part of the security community learns and competes, and CTFtime is the de-facto hub that lists events, records results, and archives what happened afterwards.

How a CTF works

FormatHow it playsExamples
Jeopardy-styleA board of independent challenges across categories, each worth points by difficulty. Solve → submit flag → points.many online events
Attack–DefenseEach team defends an identical vulnerable service while attacking the other teams' copies. Points for both.DEF CON CTF finals
Mixed / King of the HillCombines jeopardy with live, repeatedly-scored targets or territory control.qualifiers that mix formats

The first widely recognised security CTF was held at DEF CON in 1996. Some CTFs are run at conferences (DEF CON, CSAW, PlaidCTF) and many more are online-only and open to anyone.

What ctf-time.org does

CTFtime is a community calendar and archive rather than a competition itself. Its main jobs:

  • Calendar — lists upcoming CTFs with dates, formats and links so teams can plan.
  • Results & ratings — records each event's scoreboard and computes a rolling team rating per year, so you can compare teams across the globe.
  • Archive of challenges & writeups — after an event, organizers often release the challenge files, and teams publish writeups (walkthroughs) explaining their solutions. CTFtime links these together — effectively a public library of past problems and their solutions.
  • Team pages — each team has a page showing history, rankings and (often) links to their published writeups.

The "writeup archive" idea (presented responsibly)

The most educational part of the CTF culture is the writeup library: after an event ends, competitors publish step-by-step solutions. Reading old writeups is how newcomers learn the standard techniques. The shared challenge categories you'll keep meeting are:

  • Crypto — break or exploit ciphers and weak randomness.
  • Reverse engineering — dissect a binary to understand and unlock it.
  • Pwn / binary exploitation — memory-safety bugs in intentionally vulnerable programs.
  • Web — find flaws in a challenge website (injection, auth bugs, etc.).
  • Forensics — recover data from memory dumps, captures or disk images.
  • Misc / Stego — puzzles, encodings, hidden data in media.

Ethics & limits. CTF skills belong in sandboxed challenge environments. Please (1) never use "answers" to cheat in a live competition — that harms everyone and breaks its rules; (2) never point the techniques at real systems without authorisation; and (3) treat the writeup archive as a retrospective learning library, which is exactly what the community intends it to be. This page explains categories and links outward; it doesn't host working exploits for real-world targets.
