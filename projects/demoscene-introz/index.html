A small, self-contained browser gallery in the spirit of the home-computer demo & crack-intro scene. Scroll beneath the animated screen for a short history, then browse original “style” cards rendered live with the Commodore 64 palette and a browser-synthesised SID-style soundtrack. 02A BRIEF HISTORY

1982
Commodore 64 arrives
8-bit home computer with 64 KB RAM, the 6581 “SID” sound chip and the VIC-II graphics chip — cheap, popular, and a perfect canvas for young programmers.
1984–85
The crack intro appears
As games circulated, groups who removed copy-protection began adding a short credit screen — often just a scrolled name. It was the seed of a whole culture of self-taught coding, art and music.
mid-80s
From tag to showcase
Intros grew into elaborate, technically ambitious productions: raster effects, bitmap scrolls, synthesised music, loader games. Many crews made “demo-only” intros with no game attached at all.
1988–93
The demo scene matures
Across the C64, Amiga, Atari ST and PC, the emphasis shifted to pure audio-visual craft. That tradition — programming as an art form with no commercial goal — is now studied and preserved as a part of computing history.
today
Preserved and still alive
Emulation, scene archives and archives like the Internet Archive keep the software runnable, while new “intro” competitions and chiptune artists continue the craft.
